Privacy

Soundfolk has no account and no server of its own. What you listen to, like and keep stays in a database on your computer. Here is everything the app sends, to whom, and how to turn it off.

What any radio app sends

The station’s stream
To play a station, Soundfolk connects to its streaming server, which sees your IP address, like any radio player.
Searches, to Radio-Browser
The catalog is the open Radio-Browser service, searched online: what you type in Discover is sent to it.
Station logos
Loaded from the address the catalog gives, usually the station’s own website.

What you can turn off

In Soundfolk’s settings, under Privacy and Cover art.

Report my listens to Radio-Browser On
Each catalog station you start is counted in Radio-Browser’s popularity ranking, which helps small radios get found. Sent: the station’s id and, as with any request, your IP address. A notice comes with your first play, which is not counted. Stations you added yourself are never reported.
Find the track on air on the station’s website On
For streams that don’t announce their tracks, Soundfolk reads the station’s web page or its streaming server’s status, only while you listen to it. That site sees your IP address. The list of recipes saying where to look is downloaded from this site once a day.
Tell me when a new version is out On
Once a day, Soundfolk asks GitHub for the latest version number (GitHub sees your IP address). Nothing is downloaded or installed.
Covers from Deezer, iTunes or the Cover Art Archive Off
When you turn one on, the artist and title of each track on air are looked up there to show its album cover. By default, only the cover the station itself gives is shown.
Identify my liked tracks on MusicBrainz Off
When on, the artist and title of the tracks you like, never the rest of your history, are looked up in the open MusicBrainz database, for the artist, album, genre, decade and country stats.

Shared data, downloaded

Once a day, Soundfolk downloads from this site the lists curated by listeners, the stations’ donation links and the title recipes. The same files for everyone: they say nothing about you.

Links you share

A link to a station or a list carries what it shows after the # of its address: the station, the track on air, and your name only if you typed one in the settings. That part of an address is never sent to a server: this site never sees what is shared. The page asks Radio-Browser for the station, from your friend’s browser.

This website

Plain pages hosted by GitHub Pages, which sees your IP address like any web host. No cookies, no analytics, no third-party scripts. A page plays a station only when you click Listen.

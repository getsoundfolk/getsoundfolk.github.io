Contribute

Soundfolk gets better with every listener who teaches it something about a station. Most of it needs a GitHub account and no code.

Give titles to a station

Many web radios don’t say in their stream which track is playing, but show it on their website. A recipe tells Soundfolk where to read it. Added to the shared list, it gives titles to every listener of that station.

In Soundfolk, play the station, open ⓘ in the player and click Create a recipe: the app writes the file and tests it as you go. Or just Ask for a recipe there, and someone else may write it.

Help stations get donations

Many independent and community radios live on their listeners’ donations. Found the “Support us” page on a station’s website? Send it through a form: a bot checks that the station’s own site links to it, and every listener then sees it in the player.

Put together a list

A list is 5 to 40 stations picked around a theme: the community radios of a country, streams to work to, a broadcaster’s music channels. It shows in Discover with your name, and gets its own page here to share. One small text file, in a pull request.

Code, design and bug reports

Soundfolk is written in Rust and Svelte, with Tauri. Each part of the app has its specification in docs/; start there, then read the contributing guide. Contributions are under the MPL-2.0, and each commit is signed off (DCO).

Found a bug, or a station that plays badly? An issue with what you did and what you saw helps a lot.

Keep the catalog right

The stations themselves come from Radio-Browser, an open catalog anyone can edit. A wrong stream, a missing station or a bad tag is fixed there, for every app that uses it.
